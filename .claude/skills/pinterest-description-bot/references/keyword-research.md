# Pinterest Keyword Research Guide

## Why Pinterest Keyword Research Is Different
Pinterest keywords work like Google keywords, not Instagram hashtags. People TYPE what they're looking for into the search bar. Your job is to match the words your ideal reader actually uses when searching — not the words you use to describe your content internally.

---

## How to Find Great Pinterest Keywords (Free Methods)

### 1. Pinterest Search Bar Auto-Suggest (Best Free Method)
Type your topic into the Pinterest search bar and DON'T press enter. Pinterest will auto-suggest the most searched variations. These are real search terms people use.

Example: Type "content marketing" and you'll see:
- content marketing plan
- content marketing strategy
- content marketing for small business
- content marketing tips
- content marketing ideas
- content marketing checklist

Write these down — these are your keywords.

**Pro tip:** Also try typing with a space after your keyword, then the alphabet (e.g. "content marketing a", "content marketing b") to uncover more variations.

### 2. Pinterest Guided Search Bubbles
After searching a term, Pinterest shows coloured "guided search" bubbles/chips at the top of results. These are the most popular sub-topics Pinterest associates with your search. Use these as additional keyword ideas.

### 3. Look at Top-Performing Pins in Your Niche
Search your topic, filter to "Top" results, and read the descriptions of the highest-performing pins. Note what keyword phrases they use repeatedly — Pinterest's algorithm is surfacing these pins because those keywords match what people search.

### 4. Pinterest Trends Tool (Free at pinterest.com/trends)
Search any keyword to see its search volume trend over time. Useful for:
- Confirming a keyword is actually searched
- Finding seasonal peaks (to time your content)
- Discovering related trending keywords

### 5. Google Keyword Planner (Free with Google Ads account)
Pinterest and Google searches overlap significantly. Keywords that perform well on Google often perform on Pinterest too. Use Google Keyword Planner to find search volumes and related terms, then bring them to Pinterest.

## Keyword Selection Framework

### For each pin, aim to have:
- **1 primary keyword** (your main topic, medium competition, good search volume) — goes in the TITLE and first sentence of description
- **2-3 secondary keywords** (related variations, longer tail) — woven through the description
- **1-2 long-tail keywords** (very specific, lower competition, easier to rank for) — used in the middle/end of description

### Short-tail vs Long-tail:

| Type | Example | Search Volume | Competition | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short-tail | "content marketing" | Very high | Very high | Supporting keyword only |
| Mid-tail | "content marketing tips" | High | High | Primary keyword |
| Long-tail | "content marketing tips for small business owners" | Medium | Low-medium | Great for new accounts |
| Question | "how to create a content marketing plan" | Medium | Low | Great for blog post pins |

**New Pinterest accounts:** Focus heavily on long-tail keywords. Easier to rank, more targeted traffic.
**Established accounts:** Mix of mid-tail and long-tail, can target more competitive terms.

## Keyword Dos and Don'ts

### DO:
✅ Use the exact phrasing people search (not your industry jargon)
✅ Use keywords naturally in sentences — Pinterest reads for context, not just keywords
✅ Include location-based keywords if relevant (e.g. "content marketing for Australian small business")
✅ Use the same keyword in both the title AND description (reinforces the signal)
✅ Research keywords BEFORE writing descriptions, not after
✅ Vary descriptions across multiple pins to the same URL (don't duplicate)

### DON'T:
❌ Keyword stuff (e.g. "content marketing content marketing plan content marketing strategy" — this reads as spam and Pinterest may suppress it)
❌ Use hashtags (Pinterest has confirmed hashtags hurt reach)
❌ Change or paraphrase user-provided keywords — if they say "content marketing plan," use "content marketing plan" exactly
❌ Use only generic terms — be specific enough to attract the RIGHT audience
❌ Assume your industry terms = what your audience searches (e.g. your IC might search "how to get more clients" not "lead generation strategy")

## Keyword Research Prompts to Give Members

When helping a member find their keywords, ask them:

1. "What would your ideal customer type into Pinterest when they're looking for what you offer?"
2. "What problem are they trying to solve RIGHT NOW that brings them to this content?"
3. "What words do your actual customers use when they talk about their problem or desire — in their DMs, comments, or emails to you?"
4. "Is there a seasonal angle? (e.g. 'Christmas gift ideas for mum')"
5. "Is there a demographic angle that narrows it? (e.g. 'for beginners', 'for busy mums', 'for small business')"

These answers usually contain the exact keyword phrases that will perform best.

## Keyword Volume Guide (Approximate Pinterest Search Volume Indicators)

Pinterest doesn't publicly share search volume numbers, but these are general indicators:

- **High volume:** Single or two-word terms (e.g. "recipes", "home decor", "marketing")
- **Medium volume:** 3-4 word phrases (e.g. "easy dinner recipes", "home office decor")
- **Lower volume, higher conversion:** 4-6 word phrases (e.g. "easy weeknight dinner recipes for families", "small home office decor on a budget")

Lower volume keywords often convert better because the person searching is more specific about what they want — meaning if your content matches, they click.
